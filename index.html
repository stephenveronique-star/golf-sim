Setting up

Steps

  1. Put the phone on a tripod in landscape, about 3 m from the ball, facing you, with the lens at about hip height (70–80 cm) and the phone level — so your whole body, head to feet, and the ball are in the picture.
  2. Keep the phone square to the target line and level. Use Level on the camera screen to check. Turn rotation lock off (Control Centre, 🔒 button) so the app turns sideways with the phone.
  3. Slide the phone towards the net until the ball sits in the orange “BALL HERE” band on the camera screen — about a third of the way in from the side away from the net. The player still fits, and the camera sees much more of the ball’s flight.
  4. The ball should look at least 10 px on the camera screen (the “Ball … px” chip). If the app says the ball is too small, the phone is too far away — move it to about 3 m.
  5. Tap Camera, put a ball down and tap it on the screen once. That saves your tee spot (dashed green circle): from then on the app only looks for a ball there, so nothing else in the picture can fool it. When the screen turns green and says “Ready”, hit. To use a different spot, tap 📍 Tee spot saved — tap to clear.
  6. After each shot the screen goes orange — Tee up. Put the next ball in the same spot; it turns green and says “Ready” again.
  7. No sound? Check the iPhone's silent switch is off and the volume is up.
  8. Something not working? Tap the 🔧 button (or “Help me fix it” on the camera screen) and send the report and pictures to Claude.

For good readings

  • Daylight, ideally bright. A white ball against grass, a dark mat or a dark net reads best; avoid bright sky or a white wall directly behind the ball's path.
  • Keep other balls out of the picture and stand so your body is behind the ball, not between it and the phone.
  • The ball should show at 8–20 px on the camera screen. Move the phone closer if it's smaller.
  • Choose the club before you hit — spin is estimated from the club, because one camera can't see spin.
  • A shank or toe-hit for a right-hander goes toward the phone. Keep the tripod a little further back, or put a small screen in front of it, once you're comfortable with the setup.

Swing tips

After each shot the app looks at your body in the pictures (on the phone — nothing is sent anywhere) and checks: stance width and ball position for the club, head and hip sway on the backswing, weight shift at impact, your finish and your tempo (backswing time ÷ downswing time — good players are about 3 : 1). You get a swing score out of 10, the top tips, and a drill for each fault. The first time, it downloads the body-tracking files (about 20 MB). Put your height and right/left-handed in Settings › My swing. Your whole body, head to feet, needs to be in the picture — the camera screen shows “Body: head to feet in view ✓” when it is.

Replay, sequence, compare

After each shot your swing replays in slow motion by itself (turn off in Settings › My swing). It closes by itself when the next ball is ready. In the replay: Angles shows your knee, arm and spine angles; ／ Line and ∠ Angle let you draw on the picture (tap two or three points); ▦ Sequence makes one picture of the 8 swing positions you can share or save; ☆ Mark as best keeps your best swings at the top; ⚖︎ Compare puts two swings side by side, lined up at impact. The 🎥 button at the top opens all your saved swings (the last 25).

Training goal

In 🎥 My swings, pick one thing to work on (e.g. “Less hip slide”, “Tempo of 3 : 1”). The chart shows every swing — green dots are on target — with how many sessions and what percentage of swings are on target.

Phone behind the ball

On the camera screen tap 📍 Phone at the SIDE to change it to BEHIND the ball. Stand the phone about 3 m straight behind the ball, about 1 m high, pointing at your target, turned sideways. Put the ball in the orange box, with the target straight above it on the green line. Enter the phone’s height and its distance to the ball in Settings for the best numbers. From here the app measures left and right properly, plus speed and launch, as long as it sees the ball in at least three pictures before the net: leave 3 m or more between ball and net and use bright light. Tap 📷 Back camera to use the front camera, so you can see the screen while you play. You choose all this once; after that you don’t need to touch the phone. It then checks your posture (forward bend and knee flex), distance from the ball, early extension (hips moving toward the ball) and whether your head rises or dips.

What's measured and what's estimated

Ball speed and launch angle are measured from the ball's flight across the screen. Direction is estimated from how the ball's size changes as it flies toward or away from the phone, so treat left/right as a guide. Spin, club speed and curve are estimated. Carry and total come from a ball-flight model fitted to tour launch-monitor data, adjusted for your altitude and temperature.
